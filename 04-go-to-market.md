# 04 — Go-to-Market Strategy

## Primary Customer Segment: Healthcare Facilities

**Decision: A, facilities first.** The platform stays ready to support healthcare professionals and staffing agencies.

The first problem to solve is helping healthcare facilities find qualified, available professionals and fill staffing gaps faster.

## Target Facility Types

| Facility type | Why they need it |
|---|---|
| Nursing homes and skilled nursing facilities | Recurring staffing gaps and shift coverage needs |
| Home healthcare agencies | Matching caregivers, nurses and other professionals to client needs |
| Rehabilitation centers | Staffing therapists, nurses and support professionals |
| Clinics and outpatient centers | Recruiting permanent and temporary clinical staff |
| Hospitals | Larger opportunities, but more complex procurement, credentialing and integration requirements |

**Beachhead:** nursing homes, skilled nursing facilities and home healthcare providers. The pilot should show that BSM improves staffing speed, credential readiness and operational efficiency before the company pursues larger hospital systems.

## Launch Market

Options that were considered:

- **A.** Atlanta, Georgia: start locally and validate the model
- **B.** Georgia statewide
- **C.** United States nationwide
- **D.** International

**Decision: C, United States nationwide.** The platform is designed and launched for facilities across multiple states.

### Implications of a Nationwide Launch
- Credentialing must handle state-by-state licensing rules and verification sources from day one.
- The jurisdiction model (state, license type, compact licenses) is core data, not an add-on.
- Supply and demand density matters more. Concentrating early sales in a few metro areas helps avoid an empty marketplace, even with a national footprint.
